How to play
  • Mines are hidden on the board. Open every square that isn't a mine to win.
  • A number tells you how many mines touch that square (all 8 around it).
  • Mark squares you think are mines with a flag 🚩. On PC, right-click. On a phone, long-press or tap the [⛏️ Dig mode] button to switch to flag mode.
  • When a number already has that many flags around it, tap it again (double-click on PC) to open the rest around it.
  • When the game ends (win or lose), the whole board is shown: faded numbers = squares you hadn't opened, 💣 on green = mines you flagged correctly, ❌ = wrong flags. Press [View board] on the result card to see it.
  • Your first square and its neighbors are always safe.
  • Beginner 9×9 with 10 mines · Intermediate 16×16 with 40 · Expert 30×16 with 99 (tall on phones).

How to enjoy Minesweeper

Minesweeper is a logic puzzle: use the number clues to avoid hidden mines and open every safe cell. It is more about reasoning than luck, so once you can read the numbers you can clear most cells with certainty. There are three levels (Beginner, Intermediate, Expert), and it works on computers and phones.

Reading the numbers

A number tells you how many mines are in the eight cells around it. If a 1 has only one unopened neighbor, that cell is a mine. If a 1 already has a flag next to it, all its other unopened neighbors are safe. These two ideas alone solve most Beginner boards.

Patterns to know

  • 1-2-1: along the edge of the opened area, 1, 2, 1 in a row means the cells in front of both 1s are mines and the cell in front of the 2 is safe.
  • 1-2-2-1: 1, 2, 2, 1 in a row means the cells in front of the two 2s are mines and the cells in front of the 1s are safe.
  • These patterns come up often; flag them as soon as you see them and you will save a lot of time.

Tips

  • Flag only when sure. A flag is just a marker, and a wrong one will confuse you later.
  • Open around a number. When a number has as many flags as its value, double-click it (tap it again on phones) to open all its neighbors at once.
  • Stuck? Look elsewhere. Another edge of the board may give you the clue you need.
  • When you truly can't tell, compare the mines left with the cells left and choose the least likely cell. Now and then a board does come down to luck.

FAQ

Can my first click hit a mine?
No. The first cell you open and its neighbors never contain mines, so you can always start safely.
How do I place a flag on a phone?
Long-press a cell, or tap the [⛏️ Dig mode] button below the board to switch to flag mode.
Where are my records kept?
Your records for each level are saved only in this device's browser.
