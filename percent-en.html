Three common mistakes

  • Removing VAT is not × 0.9. 110,000 × 0.9 = 99,000, but the real net amount is 110,000 ÷ 1.1 = 100,000. The 10% was added to the net amount, so taking 10% off the total removes too much.
  • Up 20% then down 20% is not back to start. 10,000 up 20% is 12,000; 12,000 down 20% is 9,600, because the second step starts from the bigger number.
  • Percent vs. percentage points. An interest rate going from 3% to 4% is up 1 percentage point — but up 33% in relative terms.

Stacked discounts work the same way: 20% off and then an extra 10% off is 28% off, not 30% (0.8 × 0.9 = 0.72).

FAQ

How do I find the original price from a sale price?

Divide the sale price by (1 − discount). If 35,000 is 30% off, the original was 35,000 ÷ 0.7 = 50,000. Choose "Discount" and then "Find the original price" above.

What VAT rate should I use?

Korea's VAT is 10%, which is the default. For another country's VAT or a US sales tax, just change the tax rate box.

Are my numbers saved?

Only the last numbers for each calculation type are remembered in this browser. Nothing is sent anywhere. "Reset" brings that type back to its example.

Results are rounded to two decimal places. For tax filings, check the rounding rules that apply to you.
