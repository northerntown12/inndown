What are Renju rules?
  • Black and White take turns. The first to get 5 in a row horizontally, vertically or diagonally wins. Black moves first.
  • Because moving first is an advantage, the following moves are forbidden for Black only:
    • 3-3: a move that makes two open threes at once
    • 4-4: a move that makes two fours at once
    • Overline: a move that makes 6 or more in a row
  • Black needs exactly 5 to win; White wins with 5 or more.
  • A move that makes five for Black wins even if it is also a forbidden shape. 4-3 is allowed.
  • You have 15 seconds per move. Run out of time and you lose.

How to enjoy Omok (Gomoku)

Omok, also known as Gomoku or Five in a Row, is a game where Black and White take turns placing stones, and the first to make five in a row horizontally, vertically or diagonally wins. The rules are simple, but reading ahead makes it surprisingly deep. On WowEzBox you play on a 15×15 board with Renju rules, against the computer (easy, normal, hard) or online opponents. There is no sign-up.

Getting started

  1. Choose your stone on the right (below the board on phones): Black moves first, White second. Then choose a level.
  2. Press [Start] and tap a point on the board to place a stone.
  3. On phones, the first tap shows a preview and a second tap on the same point places the stone, so you don't misplace a move by accident.
  4. You have 15 seconds per move against the computer and 20 seconds online. The clock turns red when 5 seconds are left.

Why Renju rules?

In Omok, moving first is a big advantage, so without restrictions Black wins far more often even between equal players. Renju rules balance this by forbidding 3-3, 4-4 and overlines (six or more in a row) for Black only. White has no forbidden moves and wins with five or more. This game marks Black's forbidden points with a red ×, so you won't make a mistake even before you know all the rules. Once you are used to them, try turning the marks off.

Shapes to know

  • Open three: three stones with both ends empty. Left alone it becomes an open four next move, so block it right away.
  • Open four: four stones with both ends empty. Blocking one end still leaves five at the other, so it can no longer be stopped.
  • Four-three: one move that makes a four and an open three together. While the opponent blocks the four, the three becomes an open four. It is the classic winning move and is allowed for Black too.
  • Continuous fours: making four after four so the opponent can only block, then winning at the end. The computer on hard looks for these and blocks yours.

Tips for winning

  • Defense first. Never miss the opponent's open threes and fours; that matters more than your own attack.
  • Start near the center. The middle of the board leaves the most directions to grow.
  • Leave gaps. Stones placed one point apart are harder to block than stones placed side by side.
  • As White, use the forbidden points. If Black can only block on a forbidden point, Black can't play there and White wins.
  • Practice with Undo. Against the computer you can press [Undo] and try another move.

FAQ

Is four-three forbidden?
No. Under Renju rules only 3-3, 4-4 and overlines are forbidden for Black; four-three is allowed.
What if Black's five also forms a forbidden shape?
Exactly five in a row wins for Black. Making five comes first.
What if White makes six in a row?
White has no overline restriction and wins with five or more.
