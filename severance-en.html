Who gets severance pay in Korea

Under the Employee Retirement Benefit Security Act, employers must pay severance to anyone who meets both conditions — full-time, contract and part-time workers alike, Korean or foreign:

  • At least one year of continuous service, counted from your start date to your last working day. Even one day short means no statutory severance.
  • 15 or more contracted hours a week on average over four weeks.

It applies to workplaces of every size, including those with fewer than five employees, even if your contract does not mention it.

How it is calculated

Severance is 30 days of average wages for every year worked:

Severance = average daily wage × 30 × (days of service ÷ 365)
Average daily wage = (pay for the 3 months before leaving + yearly bonus × 3/12 + unused-leave pay × 3/12) ÷ number of days in those 3 months

  • Your leaving date is the day after your last working day. If you worked until December 31, you left on January 1.
  • The 3-month period is counted in calendar days, so it is 89 to 92 days depending on when you leave.
  • Bonuses count as 3/12 of everything paid in the last 12 months, not the full amount of a bonus paid in those 3 months. Unused-leave pay works the same way.
  • If the average wage is lower than your ordinary wage, the ordinary wage is used. On a 40-hour week, daily ordinary wage = monthly ordinary wage ÷ 209 × 8.

Example: KRW 3,000,000 a month, three years, last day July 31

Days of serviceAug 1, 2023 – Jul 31, 20261,096 days
3 months of pay3,000,000 × 3 (May 1 – Jul 31, 92 days)9,000,000

Severance97,826 × 30 × 1,096 ÷ 365≈ 8.81 million

With a KRW 3,000,000 yearly bonus, 750,000 (3/12) is added, the average daily wage becomes 105,978 and severance rises to about KRW 9.55 million.

When and how you are paid

  • Your employer must pay within 14 days of your leaving date, unless you both agree to a later date.
  • It is usually paid into an IRP (individual retirement pension) account in your name. You can close the IRP and withdraw the money; tax is withheld when you do.
  • If your company uses a DC-type pension, you receive what has built up in that account, which can differ from this estimate. A DB-type pension works much like this calculation.
  • Retirement income tax applies, but deductions for years of service make it much lower than tax on salary.
  • For disputes, the Ministry of Employment and Labor helpline is 1350 (interpretation is available).

FAQ

I worked 11 months. Do I get anything?

Not by law — statutory severance needs one full year. The calculator tells you how many more days you would need. Some companies pay under their own rules, so check your employment rules.

Why is the leaving date different from my last day?

Legally, employment ends the day after your last working day. The calculator moves it forward one day for you.

My pay rose just before I quit. Does that help?

Yes. Severance is based on the last 3 months of pay, so a raise in that period raises your severance. Unpaid leave or a pay cut in that period can lower it.
